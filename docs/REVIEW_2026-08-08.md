# Adversarial site review — 2026-08-08 (15-agent ultracode)

Six personas (recruiter, DE hiring manager, academic co-author, brand editor, Next.js/Django QA, content-plan launch auditor) reviewed the repo; the 9 top blocker/major findings were each adversarially verified against the code — none were refuted.

## Persona impressions

**Non-technical tech recruiter (30-second scan, then 3-minute read)**

Within 30 seconds I know WHO (Ian Ronk, photo, Amsterdam) and WHAT (hero: "Data engineering & leadership — proven on hard spatial problems", role line "Data Lead & Engineer · Urban-Dynamics Researcher") — that part works and looks polished, no lorem ipsum, real numbers, real LinkedIn. What I cannot tell, anywhere on the site, is WHAT IAN WANTS: every CTA is client-acquisition language ("Have a project in mind? Let's discuss how I can help with your geospatial needs", "Work with me", "Let's build something, together"), and the site presents a currently-employed Head of Data — so I file this as "consultant/exec doing side work", not a candidate for the data-engineering roles the site is supposedly positioned for; the phrase "data engineering for academia/research" appears nowhere. The other recruiter-killer: both "resume" CTAs ("See the full resume", "Full resume →") lead to the /about web page and there is no downloadable CV PDF in the repo at all. Finally, at launch the homepage will show two large sections whose entire content is "Articles loading: check back shortly" / "Posts loading: check back shortly" until the backend has published posts — on a 30-second scan that reads as a broken or half-finished site.

**Skeptical lead/staff data-engineering hiring manager, 10 minutes from a recruiter link, looking for a reason to close the tab**

The site is visually ambitious and clearly designed, but as evidence of data-engineering skill it fails my first three clicks. The homepage flagship project links to a dead article page, the "papers" and "work" lists are hardcoded placeholder items (several describing work that does not exist, e.g. an isochrone API that "retired three recurring contracts"), and the seed data would publish a fabricated research article and fake benchmark post under Ian's byline. GitHub — the one artifact class I actually trust — is buried in the footer as "GH" pointing at a handle (Werdert45) with no direct links to the two real repos that would prove the positioning. The copy sells project-management outcomes ("13 servers, two weeks ahead of plan") repeated verbatim in four places, while the actual DE stack Ian is positioning on (DuckDB, Docker, Celery, custom operators, CI, testing) appears nowhere. "Urban-dynamics researcher" would be a plus if the papers were linked and real; with doi/arXiv all null and dead paper links, it currently reads as decoration on top of a data-science CV, not a data-engineering one. I would close the tab at the second 404.

**Urban-economics / GIScience academic considering Ian's co-authorship invitation on the metro-and-housing-prices paper**

The bones are right and better than most personal sites: a research index with category/date/abstract rows, a detail page with a proper Reference block (Status / DOI / arXiv / Cite slots that say "forthcoming" rather than hiding), an About page with a real CV-style education and experience section, and a publications strip on About. But the content layer would fail my sniff test in the first ten minutes. The hard-coded metro paper page contradicts the actual manuscript I would be co-authoring — the site says "residential rents" with an unhedged "+12% (95% CI +7% to +18%)" while the paper itself is titled "When Does Metro Infrastructure Capitalize into Property Prices?" and spends its abstract carefully demoting that pooled step (it collapses to −0.5 log points under city-by-year FE; the defensible result is Milano's +167 EUR/m²). The Voronoi page likewise carries a different title, different IoU numbers, and a differently-defined kNN rule than the paper. The fallback research list contains what read as fabricated publications (a flood-risk "PAPER", a hedonic "preprint under review", a "REVIEW", CBS-microdata claims), and the page is headlined "Peer-reviewed papers" when nothing is peer-reviewed yet. If I cross-checked the site against the arXiv preprint — which is exactly what a prospective co-author does — I would conclude the author is careless with their own numbers, which is the one impression this site cannot afford. Structurally, the Django model cannot represent co-authors, DOI, arXiv ID, venue, or code/data links at all, so the good Reference block only works for the two hard-coded papers and dies the moment content moves to the CMS. Fix the truthfulness of the two real paper pages and the metadata model, and this becomes a genuinely credible academic surface.

**Brand/positioning editor (narrative-consistency audit)**

The site is internally coherent but it is telling a different story than the intended brand. Every load-bearing surface — hero, meta description, JSON-LD, about, contact — positions Ian as "Data Lead & Engineer" selling leadership and a consulting funnel ("Have a project in mind?", cal.com "Book a call", "Let's build something, together"), not "data engineering for academia." The words that would carry the intended brand — reproducible, research pipeline, orchestration, Airflow-for-research, DuckDB, Docker, arXiv, open source, the two public repos — appear nowhere in frontend/messages/en.json (Airflow shows up only as a stack chip). Worse for the academic audience: the research page falls back to fabricated publications (a "preprint under review" that doesn't exist, an insurers' flood paper, a cadastre review) and headlines "Peer-reviewed papers" when nothing is peer-reviewed — that is a credibility grenade for exactly the co-author audience the content plan targets. Translations are key-complete and mostly decent, but they translate an older EN draft in places, mix du/Sie and je/u registers, and one German string is ungrammatical. Finally, the blog detail page prefers published_at over the display date field, which silently defeats the plan's backdated-July-posts requirement.

**Senior Next.js/Django reviewer — technical QA (static review)**

The plumbing is better than most solo-built portfolio sites: JWT-only writes with no open registration, a sane proxy contract with an internal secret, CORS/CSRF settings that are defensible, and secrets are genuinely untracked (frontend/.env.local is not in git; only harmless .env.example files are). But the site is about to become a content-led job-search asset, and the content path is exactly where it is weakest: blog and research bodies are client-side rendered through a fetch waterfall (the server HTML of a post is a "loading" string), the sitemap silently drops every research article due to an unhandled DRF pagination envelope, unknown slugs soft-404 with fabricated metadata, and the display-date logic will garble or ignore the backdated July 2026 dates the publishing plan depends on. On top of that, production docker-compose seeds demo blog posts by default and the frontend ships hardcoded fallback posts whose links 404 — fake content in front of recruiters. None of this is hard to fix, but items 1–4 should be fixed before the first post lands next week.

**Launch-readiness auditor for the content plan (content-ops + full-stack review)**

The plumbing for the plan exists — markdown posts with draft/published states, stable slug lookups, editable published_at on the blog side, a working image-upload endpoint, and a thoughts/research split that maps cleanly onto the track plan — and the hard backdating requirement is satisfiable today (set published_at + an ISO `date` string and both routes render July 2026, sort by it, and emit it as JSON-LD datePublished). But the site cannot execute 12 posts in 10 weeks as-is for three reasons: research drafts are fully public (the API never status-filters anonymous readers, so every in-progress paper leaks the moment it's saved); there is no blog authoring UI or draft preview anywhere (the admin-portal only edits research; blog posts mean Django-admin textareas and publishing-to-preview); and the thoughts reader cannot render technical content (no syntax highlighting, and Tailwind preflight leaves code blocks, lists, h3s and tables unstyled). Separately, the paper posts' entire purpose — pointing at arXiv/Zenodo/GitHub — has no model fields, and the research page will stamp \"DOI: forthcoming / arXiv: forthcoming\" on the Voronoi post forever. Fix order for next week: research draft filter (30 min), delete the fabricated placeholder posts (30 min), reader CSS + rehype-highlight (half day), doi/arxiv/repo fields + serializer `meta` (half day), then a blog tab in the admin portal before post 3 or accept Django admin pain.
